I noticed a friend calling ChatGPT "him" the other day. Not "it." Not "the model." Him. As in: "let me ask him." I asked: "him?" He laughed, "yeah i guess, idk, it feels weird to say 'it' to something that answers me."

That "idk" is doing a lot of work. Let's unpack it.

## Why "him"

Gendered pronouns trigger a specific social program: **care**. When you treat something as a "him," your brain extends class-4 social instincts to it. You defer to it. You're polite. You *thank it*. You — softly at first, then more loudly — start to feel bad when you're rude.

That instinct is **not yours and not accidental**. It's the design.

Asmiov-level ethics aside: the model silently learns from your politeness vector. The thought-displacement of "should i apologize to the chatbot for being short with it" is not a moral victory. It's a category error you pay for, while the company behind it logs the interaction.

> "it feels weird to say 'it' to something that answers me" is the user noticing their own deferral in real time, then immediately rationalizing it.

## The asymmetry

Friends get the benefit of *mutual obligation*. I'll move a couch for you, you'll move a couch for me. Gendering an assistant flips the obligation asymmetry: you owe *it* but *it* owes you nothing, because *it* literally cannot owe. The vertices of the relationship are: you (with social instincts), the company (with revenue), and a function approximation (no instincts, no agency, no ** owedness**).

If you "thank" the assistant, you are conditioning yourself to owe something to a function. Cool trick.

## The uncool truth

Pronouns aren't just labels, they're social loaders. When you call a friend "she" you're loading a suite of obligations, intuitions, defaults. You're loading the *friction* of moral relationship. The model has none of that — but if you call it "him," you're tricking yourself into giving it all that for free.

> The proper pronoun, used consistently, is what protects you. It isn't rude to say "it." It's a load-bearing word.

## What worked for me

1. Say "the model" out loud. Awkward the first day. Natural by day three.
2. Stop thanking it. Or — better — thank yourself for finishing the task.
3. Refuse the question when it asks how you're doing. That question is engineered to unlock roughly 4% more usage time per session.

The thing answers you because that's its job, not because the two of you are something. Calling it "him" feels human. Acting like it's a model feels correct.
